## 1. What is claimed (verbatim formal statements)

Source: `ComparatorChallenges/NavierStokes.lean` (challenge side, two `sorry` placeholders) and `NavierStokes/ComparatorSolution.lean` (solution side, no `sorry`). The two statements are textually equal after whitespace normalisation, and Comparator's exported-statement comparison accepted them.

```lean
-- (C) whole space
theorem navier_stokes_breakdown_R3 (nu : ℝ) (hnu : nu > 0) :
    ∃ (u₀ : ℝ³ → ℝ³) (f : ℝ³ → ℝ → ℝ³),
    InitialVelocityConditionDecay u₀ ∧ ForceConditionDecay f ∧
    ¬ (∃ v p, NavierStokesExistenceAndSmoothnessRn nu u₀ f v p)

-- (D) periodic
theorem navier_stokes_breakdown_periodic (nu : ℝ) (hnu : nu > 0) :
    ∃ (u₀ : ℝ³ → ℝ³) (f : ℝ³ → ℝ → ℝ³),
    InitialVelocityConditionPeriodic u₀ ∧ ForceConditionPeriodic f ∧
    ¬ (∃ v p, NavierStokesExistenceAndSmoothnessPeriodic nu u₀ f v p)
```
(`ℝ³ = EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)`; both theorems live in `NavierStokes.Comparator`.) Permitted axioms: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; observed: exactly these.

In words: for every viscosity ν > 0 there are smooth, divergence-free, rapidly decaying (resp. periodic) data and a smooth force such that the forced Navier–Stokes system has no smooth global solution of the stated class. These are the Clay alternatives (C) and (D). The two cases are different theorems with different solution classes and are kept separate throughout.

## 2. The definitions that give the statements their meaning (READ, challenge file)

- `divergence v x := (fderiv ℝ v x).trace ℝ (ℝ^n)` — the trace of the Jacobian. `IsOnePeriodic f := ∀ x i, f (x + e_i) = f x`.
- `InitialVelocityCondition u₀`: `∀ x, ∇⬝ u₀ x = 0` and `ContDiff ℝ ∞ u₀`. (`∞` is "every finite order", not analytic; the file's `open ContDiff` scope fixes this.) `…Decay` adds `∀ m K, ∃ C, ∀ x, ‖iteratedFDeriv ℝ m u₀ x‖ ≤ C/(1+‖x‖)^K`. `…Periodic` adds `IsOnePeriodic u₀`.
- `ForceCondition f`: `ContDiffOn ℝ ∞ (↿f) (univ ×ˢ Ici 0)`. `…Decay` adds `∀ m K, ∃ C, ∀ x, ∀ t ≥ 0, ‖iteratedFDerivWithin ℝ m (↿f) (univ ×ˢ Ici 0) (x,t)‖ ≤ C/(1+‖x‖+t)^K` (K real). `…Periodic` adds `∀ t ≥ 0, IsOnePeriodic (f · t)` and the same bound with `(1+t)^K` only.
- `NavierStokesExistenceAndSmoothness nu u₀ f v p` (common core): for all `x`, `t ≥ 0`: `derivWithin (v x ·) (Ici 0) t + fderiv ℝ (v · t) x (v x t) = nu • Δ (v · t) x − gradient (p · t) x + f x t`; `∇⬝ (v · t) x = 0`; `v x 0 = u₀ x`; `ContDiffOn ℝ ∞ (↿v)` and `(↿p)` on `univ ×ˢ Ici 0`.
- `…Rn` (whole space) adds, for every `t ≥ 0`, `MemLp (‖v · t‖) 2` and `∃ E, ∀ t ≥ 0, ∫ x, ‖v x t‖² < E`. `…Periodic` adds `∀ t ≥ 0, IsOnePeriodic (v · t)` and `IsOnePeriodic (p · t)`. The periodic class has no energy condition.

## 3. Architecture of the proof

The solution file is 32 lines: each theorem is `exact` of a bridge theorem. The mathematics sits in a project-internal formulation (a "candidate" with the properties below) plus bridges to the comparator vocabulary. Closure of `ComparatorSolution`: 609 Lean modules, 382,557 lines, importing only Mathlib from outside the project (736 `import` lines), with no `sorry`, `axiom`, `unsafe`, `native_decide`, `opaque`, `implemented_by`, `extern`, `csimp`, `ofReduceBool` or `decide +kernel` outside comments (recomputed on the node, job `s437.nspkg.R1b.bind`).

### 3.1 The internal target (READ: `R3/ProblemStatement.lean`)
`CandidateProperties ν u p f K` — all hold for one fixed positive ν: `u, p` smooth on the pre-singular domain (`t ∈ [0,1)`); a single compact `K` containing the spatial supports of `u(t)` and `p(t)` for all `t ∈ [0,1)`; `f` globally smooth with compact support contained in `t > 0`; `u(0,·) = 0`; `∇·u = 0` on `[0,1)`; the equation `∂ₜu + (u·∇)u − ν Δu + ∇p = f` on `(0,1)`; `UniformFiniteEnergy (Ico 0 1) u` (square-integrable at each time, one bound for ½∫|u|²); `SpeedUnboundedAtOne u` (speed unbounded in every left neighbourhood of time one).
`GlobalFiniteEnergySolution ν f`: smooth `velocity`, `pressure` on the closed future domain, zero initial velocity, `∇·v = 0` for `t ≥ 0`, the equation for `t > 0` with the same force, and `UniformFiniteEnergy (Ici 0) v`. No support, periodicity, pressure-growth or derivative-growth assumption is placed on a competitor (docstring).
`breakdownStatement := ∀ ν > 0, ∃ u p f K, CandidateProperties ν u p f K ∧ ¬ Nonempty (GlobalFiniteEnergySolution ν f)`. The module docstring states that introducing it is not an assertion of a proof; the proof is `NavierStokesR3.theorem_1_1 : breakdownStatement` in `R3/Theorem.lean`.

### 3.2 Whole-space route (C)
1. **The viscosity-one candidate with blow-up** — `ActualCandidateAssembly.selected_witness` (1,187 lines, DOC) assembled from `ActualCandidateConstruction` (971), `ActualPrimaryCoherence` (1,942), `ActualPrimaryBounds` (1,753), `ActualPrimaryCovariance` (1,316), `CycleStateCoherence` (699), `TransitionRamp` (2,253), `SmoothFourierData` (572) and related modules (DOC: "the project's actual sums"; "the original potential, direct field, and pressure sums"). Informal content (paper §§2–9): a vortex whose leading profile is self-similar with radial scale ℓ_r ≍ τ^{1/2} and axial scale ℓ_z ≍ τ^{1/2−h} (τ = 1−t, 0 < h < 1/100), speeds |u_θ|, |u_z| ≍ τ^{−1/2−h} so the sup norm blows up as t↑1 while core kinetic energy is of order τ^{1/2−3h} → 0. The leading flow leaves a residual that cannot be a smooth force; oscillatory pulses on an auxiliary torus carry a mean momentum flux that cancels the singular part, and successive corrections remove the remaining singular errors "to every order", so the residual and all its derivatives extend smoothly through t = 1. In Lean this is the extraction `R3ActualCandidate` / `R3.ActualCandidate.selected_candidate_one_with_initial_rest` (DOC: smooth time cutoff, force support strictly inside positive time, zero data near t = 0).
2. **Localisation and force** (paper §10.1–10.2, Prop. 10.1, Lemmas 10.2–10.3; Lean `R3/ActualCandidate.lean`, 155 lines, DOC): spatial and temporal cutoffs χₓ, χₜ equal to one near (0,1); `u = curl(c𝒜) + c𝓑 e_θ`, `p = c p_loc`, so `∇·u = 0` is preserved and the support is in the compact `K`; the force is defined as the residual `f = ∂ₜu + (u·∇)u − Δu + ∇p` for t < 1, all derivatives converge uniformly as t↑1, and a Borel-type sum `Σ χ₀(b_j σ) σʲ/j! F_j(x)` extends it smoothly through t = 1 with compact support in `K × [0,2]`.
3. **Energy before the singular time** (paper Lemma 10.4): `‖u(t)‖₂² + 2∫₀ᵗ‖∇u‖₂² ≤ (∫₀ᵗ‖f‖₂)²`; in Lean, the `energy_bounded` field and `theorem_1_1_with_dissipation` (READ, statement).
4. **Comparison / uniqueness on the whole space** (paper Lemma 10.5; Lean `R3FiniteEnergyComparison.lean`, 73 lines, plus `R3/PressureFluxTest` 363, `R3/RieszTestOperators` 310, `R3/WeightedSobolev` 250, `R3/LocalizedTensorBounds` 233, `R3/PressureFluxIdentity` 176, `R3/ActualPressureFlux` 60; DOC): any smooth solution with the same force, zero data and bounded `L²` energy coincides with the candidate on every `[0,T]`, T < 1. No growth condition is available for the competitor's pressure; the proof recovers the pressure gradient through Riesz-transform identities (`π = ΣRᵢRⱼ(vᵢvⱼ − uᵢuⱼ)`) and a localised pressure-flux estimate with compact cutoffs, using only unweighted velocity energy and weighted velocity/gradient norms (docstring of `PressureFluxTest`). Consequence: a global competitor equals the candidate on `[0,1)`, hence is unbounded near t = 1, contradicting its continuity at t = 1 on the compact set `K` (the competitor is smooth on the closed half-line). In Lean: `compact_candidate_excludes_global_solution`, `hc.no_global_solution_one`.
5. **Arbitrary viscosity** (`R3/ViscosityScaling`, `R3/Theorem.lean`, READ for the use): from ν = 1 to ν > 0 by the spatial rescaling `u_ν(t,x) = √ν u(t, x/√ν)`, `p_ν = ν p(t, x/√ν)`, `f_ν = √ν f(t, x/√ν)` (time is not rescaled, so the singular time stays 1; the equation, support, smoothness, energy bound and blow-up are preserved — check by direct substitution: ∂ₜu_ν = √ν ∂ₜu, (u_ν·∇)u_ν = √ν (u·∇)u, νΔu_ν = √ν Δu, ∇p_ν = √ν ∇p). `theorem_1_1_with_initial_rest` also records that `u = p = 0` for `|t| ≤ 3/8`.
6. **Bridge to (C)** (READ: `R3/ComparatorBridge.lean`, `ComparatorR3Theorem.lean`, `ComparatorR3Bridge.lean`): `comparator_of_breakdown` takes `CandidateProperties ν u p f K` and `¬ Nonempty (GlobalFiniteEnergySolution ν f)` and returns the data `u₀ = 0`, `f' = toComparator f`.
   - Data: `zero_initial_condition_decay` (divergence of a constant is 0, `contDiff_const`, decay with C = 0). `forceConditionDecay_of_compact`: from `f` smooth with compact support, put `K' = snd '' tsupport f` (compact), a time bound `|t| ≤ max M 0 + 1` on the support, and apply `CompactSpatialForceDecay.forceConditionDecay` — space–time decay for every real exponent from compact spatial and future-time support.
   - Non-existence: given a comparator solution `(v,p)` for these data, `globalSolutionOfComparator` builds a `GlobalFiniteEnergySolution ν f`: smoothness by `fromComparator_smooth`; the equation by `comparator_equation_Rn`, which rewrites `derivWithin`/`fderiv`/`Δ`/`gradient` into the project's `temporalDerivative`/`advection`/`spatialLaplacian`/`pressureGradient` (checking the coordinate swap `(t,x) ↔ x t`); divergence via `divergence_eq`; zero initial value; energy: from `∃ E, ∀ t ≥ 0, ∫‖v x t‖² < E` and `MemLp 2`, via `memLp_two_iff_integrable_sq_norm`, the bound `max 0 (E/2)` for the kinetic energy ½∫‖v‖². This contradicts the hypothesis.
   - `ComparatorR3Theorem.navier_stokes_breakdown_R3` is `theorem_1_1 ν hν` followed by `comparator_of_breakdown`. (The same file also contains a further theorem `option_C_of_compact_candidate`; the proof of `navier_stokes_breakdown_R3` in that file does not reference it. Whether any other module uses it was not checked; it cannot affect the checked statement.)

### 3.3 Periodic route (D) (READ: `PeriodicPaperTheorem.lean` statement part, `ComparatorTheorem.lean`, `PeriodicPaperComparator.lean`; DOC for `PeriodicViscosity`, `MaximalLifespan`)
- The torus is represented by spatially 1-periodic functions on ℝ³. `PeriodicPaper.CandidateProperties` requires periodic `u`, `p` (on `[0,1)`) and `f` (on `t ≥ 0`), `K` compact inside the fundamental interior, supports intersected with the fundamental cube inside `K`, zero initial velocity, `f` smooth with compact future time support and zero for `t ≤ 0`, the equation on `(0,1)`, and `SpeedUnboundedAtOne`.
- `PeriodicPaper.GlobalSmoothSolution ν f` (competitor): smooth `velocity`, `pressure` on the closed future domain, both periodic in space, zero initial velocity, divergence-free, the equation with force `f`. No energy or pressure normalisation.
- `periodic_corollary : breakdownStatement` (READ): from `theorem_1_1_with_initial_rest` obtain a whole-space candidate that is at rest near t = 0; `exists_compression_scale` and `ParabolicScaling.compressedCandidate` compress it (an affine parabolic clock that preserves singular time one) so that all supports lie in the central quarter-cube |xᵢ| ≤ 1/4; `of_compact_candidate` periodises by a lattice sum after discarding the arbitrary post-time-one extensions (`beforeOne`). Exclusion of a periodic competitor: `PeriodicViscosity.excludes_global_solution` (periodic classical uniqueness identifies the competitor with the candidate on every compact interval before time one; its uniform boundedness across time one, by compactness and continuity, contradicts unbounded speed — DOC). The docstring of `MaximalLifespan` states it assumes no general Navier–Stokes existence theorem and never identifies pressure gauges.
- Bridge: `option_D_same_force_of_paper_candidate`: data `u₀ = 0`, `f' = toComparator f` (argument order only; neither viscosity nor time is rescaled); `InitialVelocityConditionPeriodic` by `zero_initial_condition`; `ForceConditionPeriodic` from `CandidateConsequences.futureJet_decay` (the periodic force has compact future time support, hence decay of every order in `t`); non-existence by `periodicPaperSolution_of_comparator`, which converts a comparator periodic solution into a `GlobalSmoothSolution` (module `PeriodicComparatorSolution`, not read line by line).

## 4. Where the weight of the argument sits
The analytic core is the construction of the viscosity-one candidate (item 3.2.1), 10,693 lines of Lean in the eight modules named in item 1 (more in modules not named) and a 166-page informal paper. This account does not re-derive it and does not certify the paper; the formal claim is the kernel acceptance of the two declarations against the challenge statements. The remaining steps (localisation, energy, comparison, scaling, periodisation, bridges) are as described, with the bridges and statement-level definitions read in full for this package.
